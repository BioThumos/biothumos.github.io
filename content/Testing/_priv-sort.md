## ***Serg’s opinion on what to test***

* Determine whether I still have Lyme and co-infections. I am still researching which tests to do. Seems like Igenex \+ B16+ \+ perhaps Nanotrap antigen \+ perhaps Armin (though hearing there are too many false positives)

\#\#\# Igenex is great for confirmation if possitive (high specificity) but doesn't rule out Lyme if negative. But worth it if you had a positive IgG. 

\#\#\# B16 this is I believe an HLA genotype? I would not do it. You cannot change your genetics, plus, I don't think it will give you much info. However the HLAs to know if you are susceptible to mold I think might be important to know. 

* EBV: here I'm thinking Laborwick saliva PCR, and a selection from IFT/LTT/EliSpot. 

I don't know which yet, still learning about testing.

\#\#\# As for Epstein-Barr testing, I personally think that the standard IgGs for EBNA, VCA, EA, and gp350 are more than sufficient, bearing in mind that most of us with ME/CFS have abortive viral replication. That means we are not forming complete viral particles, so simply knowing that your body has been exposed to the virus at some point is enough to recognize it as a problem. In addition, since EBV is present in about 95% of the general population and possibly in close to 100% of people with ME/CFS, we can reasonably assume it is part of our pathophysiology.

\#\#\# Anyway, if I had to choose, and given that your case seems rather inflammatory — meaning your immune system is capable of producing antibodies and mounting responses to antigens — I think the ELISPOT (the LTT) is a much better option than a PCR. A negative PCR doesn’t give you any useful information, and getting a positive PCR is very difficult.

* Rest of the herpes family. Know even less about this than testing EBV.

\#\#\#As for the other herpesviruses, I think the same way. For example, if you had access to a stomach biopsy, I would suggest doing a PCR for HHV-6 — it would quite possibly come back positive, but in blood it is very hard to catch. Therefore, I would just do the standard IgM and IgG antibody tests. And I would simply assume, even if they don’t show up, that HHV-6 is part of the problem, since the same thing happens with this virus. It integrates into the ends of the DNA, producing an abortive replication that you don’t see and that your immune system usually does not detect — but we know it is part of the pathophysiology of ME/CFS. I would say for the vast majority of patients.

* Entero: Virologie Frankfurt's neutralization antibody tests were recommended.

\#\#\# I’m not familiar with this test, nor can I advise you on the best test for enteroviruses, since I haven’t studied it in depth.

* CMA/CNA: Vibrant Wellness/SpectraCell/Cell Systems Sciences

\#\#\#Honestly, I think you can skip this test and rather do the mitochondrial test I recommend below. As you know, in ME/CFS you are going to have many nutritional deficiencies, either due to malabsorption — from leaky gut, low stomach acid, and so on — or because the immune system is highly activated and therefore consumes nutrients. And then, at the Redox level, possibly at the beginning of the illness, when you are in the more CDR1 or pro-inflammatory state, you may even have elevated Nrf2 or antioxidant enzymes like glutathione peroxidase, but over time they become depressed and you will find antioxidant levels to be low.

But this test does not really give you useful information and, honestly, if you simply take a proper multimineral without iron, cover magnesium in a way that it is absorbed — whether through Epsom salts or with DMSO — and take the nutrients of the methylation cycle, namely active B9 and B12, confirming in the OAT that methylmalonic acid (for B12 status) decreases and that FIGLU (for active folate status) is also normalized, I think you are covering practically everything you need, and this test, in my opinion, is not necessary.

* Biovis: stimulated cytokines for measuring Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg status \+ possibly some other tests from their catalogue

\#\#\#Absolutely yes. Make sure that the three regulatory populations include the CD25 marker in addition to FoxP3, and that they are measuring TGF-β1, ideally also prostaglandin E2, as well as interferon-alpha and IL-12. In addition, the TH2 cytokines such as IL-10, IL-5, IL-13, etc., but especially IL-10 and TGF-β1.

It is also very important, if they have the capability, to measure CD3⁻/CD57⁺ cells as a marker of immunosuppression and NK cell status. This can serve as an indirect but, in my opinion, very valid marker to help confirm Lyme.

* Any more suggestions of tests you find valuable are welcome. Perhaps testing current CDR state would be interesting, but I don't yet know much about it.

\#\#\#In my opinion, ME/CFS derives from an immunological problem that cannot cope with chronic infections and with sterile vicious cycles that perpetuate the illness — whether at the immunological level, at the level of cell membranes, or at the neurological level. At the cellular level, this translates into mitochondrial failure, which is not due to structural damage but rather to the mitochondria themselves deliberately downregulating, misperceiving a constant danger signal. Therefore, to break out of this state, one must either stimulate the immune system directly or improve mitochondrial function so that the immune system can recover.

Therefore, knowing the mitochondrial status is fundamental, and if what you truly want is to confirm the disease, to confirm the mitochondrial failure, and perhaps to have a baseline for assessing whether the treatments you are undergoing — even if you do not yet feel them clinically — are nevertheless working behind the scenes, then the following mitochondrial profile seems to me absolutely essential for that purpose.
