**My leading model is: an NAD-triggered redox/ion-handling disturbance became self-sustaining through sensory–neuroimmune feedback, with autonomic dysfunction and limited cellular repair lowering the threshold for further crashes.** The hypotheses below separate the _trigger_, _maintenance mechanisms_ and _treatable amplifiers_.

## 1. Redox–ion-handling feedback — strongest hypothesis for the original transition

**Proposed pathway:**  
NAD perturbation → respiratory electron-handling mismatch/ROS → NAD-consuming enzymes and ADP-ribose → **TRPM2 Ca²⁺ entry** → increased ion-pumping demand → poorer Na⁺/Ca²⁺ regulation and glutamate clearance → further ROS/excitability. TRPM2–extrasynaptic NMDAR coupling provides an experimentally demonstrated amplification mechanism. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35421327/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Patient fit:** initial improvement followed by collapse; subsequent intolerance of NAD+, NADH, KL1333, methylene blue and SkQ1. Because interventions pushing redox in different directions caused overlapping symptoms, I favour **instability of flux/signalling over a simple permanently elevated NADH/NAD+ ratio**.

**Genetic modifiers:** CACNA1A could affect calcium handling; KCNT1 could affect the response to intracellular sodium and NAD+. These are parallel mechanisms—not a linear KCNT1→TRPM2 pathway. Experimental NAD+ modulation of Slack/KCNT1 is real. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19386908/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Treatment implication:** interrupt downstream excitation/inflammation first. **Do not repeat NAD/KL1333/MB challenges merely to test this hypothesis.**

## 2. Trigeminal and peripheral neurogenic inflammation — strongest fit for sensory pain

**Proposed pathway:**  
Sensory input → trigeminal/thalamic amplification → **CGRP and substance-P/NK1 signalling**; peripheral **macrophage NOX2 → Schwann-cell TRPA1/NOX1 → oxidants → nociceptor activation** supplies a persistent pain loop. The latter circuit has been demonstrated experimentally. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29192190/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Patient fit:** photophobia, phonophobia, tinnitus escalation, forehead/nape pain, immediate nasal “lightning-bolt” pain, corrosive limb sensations and conscious light-triggered distress. This explains more than an epilepsy-centred model.

**Practical options:**

- **Rimegepant**, if the headache assessment supports a migraine component. Improvement in head pain/photophobia with unchanged systemic PEM would identify a _treatable component_, not necessarily the whole illness. His cold/blue extremities and CYP3A interactions require particular attention. [Pfizer Labeling](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=19036)
- **Prescribed topical lidocaine on a focal painful limb area**, not intranasally or over large areas: a lower-systemic-exposure way to assess how much peripheral afferent activity contributes. Local relief would favour a peripheral component. [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=eb503f1f-cd3a-ab76-e053-2995a90ab1c8&version=3&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 4. Neurovascular/autonomic mismatch — substantial co-driver

**Proposed pathway:**  
Impaired vascular/autonomic compensation ± endothelial NO dysfunction → inadequate perfusion during posture, meals or cognitive demand → adrenergic compensation → further sensory activation.

**Patient fit:** POTS, dependent discoloration, cold extremities, positional visual dimming and **heart-rate improvement without broader improvement on ivabradine**. My inference: tachycardia is probably partly compensatory or secondary, not the principal lesion.

**The important treatment fork:**

|Dominant physiology|Candidate direction|
|---|---|
|Pooling/low preload, inadequate vasoconstriction|Compression/volume strategy; **midodrine**|
|Primary sympathetic overactivity with BP surges|**Guanfacine**|
|Unclear mixture|Record BP/HR during ordinary tolerated postures and spontaneous symptoms before choosing either|

Midodrine and guanfacine have evidence in different POTS subgroups. **Standing tachycardia alone cannot choose between them.** Guanfacine also needs particular caution with existing bradycardic medication. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23978222/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 5. Compartmental antioxidant/repair bottleneck — plausible maintenance mechanism

**Two compatible components:**

**5A. Mitochondrial protection:** reduced mitochondrial glutathione import/recycling or protein-folding reserve → oxidant-sensitive respiration. **SLC25A39 T122I/F239L and HSPD1 E335K** are modifier candidates. SLC25A39 deficiency can selectively reduce mitochondrial glutathione while sparing whole-cell levels—explaining why systemic supplementation might miss the relevant compartment. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04025-w)

**5B. Inadequate recovery:** disrupted **mTORC1–4E-BP translation, ISR/ER stress, lysosomal disposal and PINK1/Parkin turnover** prevent return to baseline after insults.

**Patient fit:** persistent step-downs following rapamycin/bezisterim, rather than uniformly beneficial responses to “anti-inflammatory” or mitochondrial treatments. mTORC1 supports mitochondrial protein translation as well as suppressing autophagy; inhibiting it can therefore remove useful compensation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24206664/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Implication:** I favour **reducing the load on repair systems over forcing biogenesis/autophagy**. The slight RNA GSR increase and PPARGC1B reduction receive supporting—not decisive—weight. I would not repeat generic mitochondrial panels or restart rapamycin to adjudicate this.

## 6. Persistent immune input — two nonexclusive upstream hypotheses

**6A. Antigen-driven:** persistent viral material or gut-derived microbial products → **TLR/IRF/NF-κB signalling** → chemokines, sensory sensitisation and metabolic inhibition.

**6B. Sterile immune-driven:** receptor-directed antibodies/immune complexes → **GPCR dysfunction, FcγR–Syk/complement activation**, potentially including small-fibre/autonomic injury. These are distinct from the biochemists’ stronger claim that all patients require global immune stimulation. Their notes explicitly propose both antigen-dependent and sterile loops. Pasted text (2)

**Treatment priority:** **LDN** is a reasonable broader symptom trial before further immune-stimulant or antiviral combinations; observational long-COVID studies report improvement in pain, fatigue and PEM. It is not a selective test of TLR4 or any particular immune mechanism, and requires an opioid-free medication context. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37804660/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Escalation discriminator:** skin biopsy for small-fibre density becomes worthwhile **if its result would change access to specialist immunotherapy**. Post-COVID SFN has preliminary IVIG-response evidence, whereas the randomized autoimmune-POTS trial did not outperform albumin. I would not jump directly to IVIG or B-cell depletion from the present profile. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38630952/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Compatibility versus genuine conflicts

**Compatible:** H1–H6 can reinforce one another. Redox stress can sensitise nerves; nerve signalling can activate immune cells; immune mediators can impair perfusion and repair. Viral persistence and sterile autoimmunity can coexist.

**The important conflicts are _directional_, within the same tissue and time:**

|Competing explanations|Consequence|
|---|---|
|**Excess NADH versus inadequate NADPH/GSH defence**|**Not mutually exclusive.** Different pools and compartments; more reducing substrate need not improve protection. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26256392/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)|
|**mTOR overactivity versus inadequate mTOR-supported translation**|Opposite treatment implications. His rapamycin response shifts my preference away from further empirical mTOR suppression.|
|**Blocked autophagy initiation versus blocked lysosomal disposal**|Can coexist, but stimulating initiation alone may not solve disposal.|
|**Primary sympathetic excess versus compensatory sympathetic drive**|Sympatholysis may help the former and worsen the latter.|
|**KCNT1/CACNA1A gain versus loss of function**|Opposite channel interventions; the gene names cannot select a drug.|
|**Sensory hyperactivity versus subsequent network suppression**|Can occur sequentially: intense input first, cognitive/sleep-pressure shutdown afterward. They need not be contradictory.|

## My practical order

**First:** choose **cromolyn/ketotifen versus LDN** according to whether allergic/GI flares track the broader illness.  
**Second:** separately assess a **migraine/neurogenic-pain treatment**, rather than treating the episodes as seizures.  
**Third:** resolve the **autonomic treatment direction** before adding guanfacine, midodrine or vasodilators.

Use one prescriber-guided change at a time. **Improved itching alone identifies an amplifier; improved sensory tolerance plus reduced next-day PEM suggests a more central driver.** Judge benefit at comparable activity—not by increased stimulation, sedation, or willingness to push through symptoms.
