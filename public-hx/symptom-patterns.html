This was the predominant PEM/PENE pattern before the October 2024 NADH-associated change and still occurs, although other PEM presentations are now more prominent. It may include brain burning, cognitive slowing, helmet-like pressure, and sometimes tinnitus, perceptual lag/instability or irritability. Not every feature must occur in every episode.

The diary still refers to PENE in July 2026. The overview descriptions do not establish a total number of PENE episodes, so no standalone rate is shown.

Brain-fatigue / sleep-pressure crash

Crash presentation, often followed by sleep

A central-shutdown crash may begin with worsening Brain fatigue, sometimes with the patient-described lactate-like brain sensation. It can develop into marked sleep pressure and sometimes a crash into sleep.

The patient describes this form as relatively "clean": an abrupt loss of thinking capacity and strong sleep pressure can occur without severe systemic pulsing/tingling. Sleep is common, not inevitable; some crashes do not result in sleep. Brain fatigue or lactate-like sensations without a full crash do not establish this subtype.

Poisoned-type crash

Crash presentation with a poisoned quality

Some severe crashes involve a poisoned quality and overwhelming systemic malaise, intense, sometimes painful deep tingling/pulsing especially in brain and spine, and occasionally severe internal hammering or tremors. Abrupt cognitive slowing and inability to initiate movement can accompany them. Episodes have been reported after sexual release or intense cognitive/emotional activity, but these associations are not proven causes.

Poisoned feeling alone does not establish that a full Crash occurred.

Recurring evening-type sensory pattern

This sensory experience is tracked independently of exertion-triggered PEM. Its traditional name describes how it was originally noticed, not a fixed time of day.

Evening-type sensory waves

Recurring sensory pattern; exertion trigger not required

Earlier diary entries call this “evening PEM”; later entries also say “evening pulsing” or “evening tingling”. The patient describes a diffuse, internally palpable wave-like sensation, less sharp than regular deep tingling and not heartbeat-synchronous or a true vibration. The pattern probably occurs on many days, but its frequency is not established by the diary. It can appear in the morning or afternoon, despite the name.

The patient dates its emergence to the period after NADH in late 2024; the exact first occurrence cannot be recovered. Named descriptions appear in archive correspondence by February 2025 and in the diary from August 2025. The later wording is directly linked in the records. The pattern does not necessarily follow exertion or constitute a PEM crash. It can co-occur with PEM, sleep pressure, and other symptoms.

Similar presentation during the SAG trial

During a January 2026 trial of S-acetyl-L-glutathione (SAG), the patient reported stronger, deep, mostly non-painful tingling and pulsing resembling the earlier evening pattern. He subsequently described this as the familiar sensation returning, rather than a new symptom. During this period, sinus and behind-the-eye pressure was also prominent; however, sinus pressure had started before SAG, and the patient did not establish a causal link.

The patient distinguished this wave-like sensory presentation from the dull, lactate-like brain/neck sensation described after bezisterim. A similar forehead/retro-orbital and whole-body pulsing presentation was reported again during a different treatment trial in April 2026. These similarities do not establish a common biological mechanism.

  • 28 January 2026 diary: strong tingling described as possibly evening PEM, with severe sinus/eye pressure documented the same day (S2-OBS-4694; S2-OBS-4699).
